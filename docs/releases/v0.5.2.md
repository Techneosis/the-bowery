## What's new: a restarted pull loop picks its running jobs back up

What a surface leaves running for a job (a watcher that follows its agent)
lives in the pull loop's process tree. A service manager that ends the
loop's whole tree when it restarts the loop (systemd's
`KillMode=control-group`) ended those too, and the job's record was left
pointing at a process group with nothing in it: the job looked orphaned
until it ended some other way.

- **`attach`**, a new optional surface command. When `office pull --loop`
  starts, before its first pass, it runs `attach` for every running job
  whose process group is gone, with the job on stdin and `OFFICE_JOB_STATE`
  in its environment. What `attach` leaves running in its group becomes the
  job's group: the record's `pgid` is rewritten (so the owner's stop ends
  the right processes) and `reattached` counts the times. It's logged
  `reattached`, or `attach-failed` with smoke, in which case the job stays
  as it was.
- A surface without `attach` is left alone: its agent may live outside the
  group, so an empty group says nothing about the job.
- A job that ends while it is being attached has the new group ended too.

An office host older than this one refuses a machine file with `attach` in
it (an unknown key), so upgrade the office host before adding it.

### Tests

- The CI suite runs its tests in parallel (`scripts/test-suite`), and
  `scripts/ci-local` runs it here and on a machine of the other platform at
  once. Timing waits are bounded by the timer they rule out, not by a
  machine's speed.
